## 4. Null and structural controls (100 layouts, sigma 0.15)

- **Shuffled labels.** Scoring the same predictions against randomly permuted labels gives 24.6% [23.6, 25.5] (2455 of 10000); chance is 25%.
- **Permutation null for the calibration J.** With the in/out labels of the 200 calibration probes shuffled, the best in-sample Youden J is 0.076 [0.068, 0.084] (maximum 0.170 over 100 layouts), so J near 1 is not what calibration produces by chance.
- **Global phase.** A stored pattern multiplied by a constant phase has |overlap| 1.000000 to 1.000000 with the original (1000 draws): |overlap| is blind to a global phase, as the README says.

|---|---|---|---|
| real toy (reference) | 99.91% [99.83, 99.97] | 99.64% | 99.55% [99.37, 99.71] |
| pure phase waves, no envelope | 98.50% [98.08, 98.89] | 99.75% | 98.30% [97.88, 98.70] |
| random wiring (envelope kept) | 99.97% [99.93, 100.00] | 99.64% | 99.61% [99.47, 99.74] |
| four random fields as patterns | 100.00% [100.00, 100.00] | 99.75% | 99.75% [99.64, 99.85] |

Mean |overlap| between different stored patterns: 0.137 (max 0.799) for enveloped waves, 0.335 (max 0.949) for pure phase waves.

**Pure phase control.** The README and DESIGN.md say pure phase waves were falsified in membrane-heart (recognition 0.325) and that the envelope is 'a precondition'. In this toy, pure phase waves are recognised at 98.5% (no gate). The 0.325 does **not** reproduce here: the pure phase patterns of different seeds have a mean |overlap| of 0.335 (max 0.949), so |overlap| tells them apart. The mechanism the claim rests on (global-phase blindness) is real (above), but it does not bite in this graph. The envelope lowers the pattern overlaps (0.14 against 0.34) and slightly improves recognition (99.9% against 98.5%), so it helps modestly; it is not a precondition here.

**Layout independence.** Recognition is 99.97% with random wiring and 100.00% with four random fields that have no layout at all, against 99.91% for the real toy. The recognition and refusal results therefore say nothing specific about the brain-shaped geometry; they are generic to a four-pattern outer-product memory in 128 dimensions. Only the spread, split and cut statistics (sections 6 and 7) depend on the geometry.

- **Intact graph:** the fraction of right-hemisphere neurons a left wave never reaches is 4.9% [2.3, 7.9]; 185 layouts reach every right neuron and 15 do not. The intact graph has more than one connected component in 21 layouts (at seed 42 the right-front experience starts in a 22-neuron component that no left wave can enter, even with the cross edges present).
- **After removing the cross edges** the unreachable fraction is 100% in every layout (minimum over layouts and seeds 1.000). This is true by construction (no edge crosses), so it is not a finding. It is reported as the fraction that the README's 'right side unreachable' refers to.

## 7. Recognition with the cross edges removed (measured only)

100 layouts per envelope width, sigma 0.15, 100 probes per layout per condition. 'Cut' = the intact graph with its cross edges deleted; the same four start neurons are used. (a) intact memory, intact probes; (b) memory stored on the intact graph and frozen gate, probes are waves from the cut graph; (c) memory stored after the cut with its own gate. The width is the envelope parameter (default 12). 'Opposite hemisphere energy' is the share of an intact stored wave's energy that lies in the hemisphere it did not start in. This describes a graph operation in a toy. It is not a model of any condition.

| width | (a) accepted and correct | (b) cut probes, intact memory | (c) cut, re-stored | mean overlap intact vs cut wave | opposite hemisphere energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 99.57% [99.42, 99.70] | 99.45% [99.29, 99.60] | 99.53% [99.38, 99.67] | 0.995 | 1.0% |
| 24 | 99.48% [99.33, 99.61] | 97.62% [97.18, 98.04] | 99.43% [99.26, 99.58] | 0.920 | 15.0% |
| 48 | 98.51% [98.18, 98.84] | 87.85% [86.60, 89.04] | 99.26% [99.06, 99.45] | 0.795 | 36.2% |

At the default width (12) the cut changes the stored waves very little (mean overlap 0.995; about 1.0% of their energy lies across the midline) and recognition is statistically unchanged from (a) to (b): 99.57% to 99.45%. With a very wide envelope (width 48) the waves cross the midline (36% of the energy), the cut waves overlap the intact ones less (0.79), the frozen gate refuses more of them, and accepted-and-correct falls from 98.5% to 87.8% while the ungated argmax stays at 99.3%. So the cut only matters here once the envelope is wide enough to reach across it; that is a property of the wave envelope chosen in this toy.

## 8. What was falsified, negative or surprising

1. **Pure phase waves were recognised at 98.5% in this toy**, so the 0.325 quoted from membrane-heart was not reproduced. This is a different construction, so it neither confirms nor refutes membrane-heart; it does mean that the envelope is not shown to be a precondition here.
2. **Recognition does not depend on the brain-shaped layout** (random wiring 99.97%, random patterns 100.00%).
3. **J = 1.000 is easy separability**, in sample; held-out J is 0.994 and no tau reaches a mean J of 1.
4. **The gate does not refuse structured unfamiliar input**: 49.8% of waves from other neurons were refused.
5. **'Unreachable after the cut' is true by construction**, and `coupling=False` is a re-wiring, not a cut.
6. **The intact graph is sometimes disconnected** (21 of 200 layouts), which makes some stored experiences live in a fragment.
7. **Number of cross edges does not predict the median delay** (rho -0.02 [-0.16, 0.12]).
8. **The cut leaves recognition unchanged at the default envelope width**, and only matters for wide envelopes.
9. The gate calibrated at sigma 0.15 refuses true patterns well before the argmax fails: at sigma 0.30 it refuses 69% of true patterns while the ungated argmax is still 90% correct (accuracy among accepted 97.7%).

## 9. Honest limits

- Not a brain, not neuroscience, not a medical device, not a diagnosis. Nothing here transfers to a real brain or a real person, and none of the split or cut results has a clinical reading.
- Synthetic and seeded: the rates describe this toy's random layouts and generators (isotropic Gaussian noise and random fields). Other noise models and other out-of-distribution families would give other answers (section 5 shows one).
- Sigma is not comparable to any physical noise level; at 128 components, sigma 0.15 is a low signal-to-noise probe.
- The probes within a layout are correlated; the seed bootstrap interval is the appropriate one and the Wilson interval is shown only for reference. With 100 to 200 layouts the seed intervals are themselves approximate.
- The geometry is two random ellipses with k-nearest-neighbour edges. Seed 42 happens to be a layout in which one start neuron sits in a small disconnected fragment, so single-layout statements (the README demo) are not typical of all layouts.
- Layout seeds 0 to 99 are shared by several experiments, and layout seed 42 is among them. Nothing was tuned on these seeds, but the experiments are not independent of each other.
- The tau grid step is 0.01 in calibration and 0.02 in section 5; J values are therefore quantised.
- No test of plasticity, learning or time dynamics exists in this package, so none is claimed.
